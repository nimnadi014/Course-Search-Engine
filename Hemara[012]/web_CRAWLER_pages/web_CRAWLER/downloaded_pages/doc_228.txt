Frequently asked questions What does a life coach do exactly? A life coach develops custom strategies to help others achieve personal fulfillment. They accomplish this with a number of exercises, including defining priorities in professional and personal spheres, setting goals, identifying obstacles and ways to overcome them, and developing actionable plans that lead to a meaningful existence. Some life coaches also assist those dealing with life transitions. People in all stages and walks of life benefit from the services of skilled life coaches, including students, new parents, those struggling with choosing a career or switching to a new path, and those wishing to realize a lifelong dream such as starting their own business. A good life coach breaks up the big picture into manageable pieces and helps clients craft realistic game plans to find their sweet spots in life. Who is best suited for a career in life coaching? A career as a life coach is best suited for anyone who has a passion for personal development and wants to share that passion with others. This line of work requires an empathic approach. Life coaches help people achieve their personal and professional goals and use different techniques to help their clients self-reflect. While life coaches are often referred to as mentors, the purpose of a life coach is not to give direct advice. Instead, life coaches should help their clients find the strategies and solutions that will break down barriers in their personal lives or careers. Life coaching is a mix of holding clients accountable with goal-oriented tasks while still offering positive and empathic support each step of the way. If you have strong people skills and are excited about the prospect of being self-employed or working for a privately owned coaching firm, consider becoming a life coach. What skills or experience should I have before learning life coaching? Many life coaches have earned a bachelor’s degree or a master’s degree in communication studies, sociology, or psychology, but it isn’t necessary to obtain that educational background to become a life coach. Effective life coaches are also excellent communicators and active listeners. While you can become a successful non-certified life coach, clients may be skeptical of life coaches without an actual accreditation or certification. Earning a life coach certification is an excellent way to differentiate yourself from other life coaches. Obtaining certification in a niche, such as spirituality or wellness, is also a key differentiator. When honing in on the skills you should enhance and what experiences you should gain to help clients in your niche, find something that aligns with your hobbies, interests, and background to speak confidently and passionately about it. What are some common career paths for a life coach? Life coaches can go into several different careers, such as relationship coaching, career coaching, or spiritual coaching. Life coaches can help their clients deal with personal issues like divorce or professional issues like advancing a career. Each area has its specialty. While health and wellness coaches guide their clients on improving health and vitality, executive and leadership coaches help their coachees manage and develop their managerial skills. If you are unsure which specific niche you should pick for your career path, decide what interests you by looking at your hobbies and background. For example, if you’ve been a business leader and want to help your clients with stress management, consider becoming a career coach. At any point, you can also decide to change your niche if you discover your particular passions lie elsewhere. What types of places hire people with experience in life coaching? Consulting firms and other organizations actively hire life coaches. These companies seek out career coaches to help their employees with mental and emotional management tools. Companies need to ensure their employees are healthy and productive, so professional and personal development coaches have never been more in demand. Professional development and executive coaching platforms seek coaches to provide global corporate training. Many corporate divisions have their own internal career coaches and other privately owned coaching companies that hire life coaches. Recruitment agencies look for career planners to help coach current executives and new graduates. Sports teams and legal firms hire coaches to facilitate team-building exercises. High-level executives (VPs, CEOs, etc.) privately hire coaches to assist with personal coaching for staff relationships and communication issues. Many life coaches also start their own businesses. What is the difference between a life coach and a therapist? Therapy and life coaching share a common objective in creating positive changes in a person’s life, but significant differences exist between the two roles. Life coaches design actionable plans to help their clients clarify their priorities and achieve their goals. Therapists, on the other hand, work with clients to explore painful emotions and traumas. They tend to have an introspective focus on the past, with the intention of resolving those issues to prevent the repetition of destructive patterns. Life coaching differs, as it focuses on the future to construct a fulfilling and meaningful life. Another key difference is that life coaches don’t diagnose, offer clinical treatment, or otherwise address issues such as anxiety and depression from a healthcare perspective. The main focus of therapy is healing, while the main focus of life coaching is achieving defined goals.
